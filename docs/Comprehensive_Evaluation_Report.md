# Comprehensive Evaluation Report: Forecasting 6G Traffic using Generative Foundation Models

## Overview
This report reviews the results generated by the `notebooks/04_full_evaluation.ipynb` pipeline, comparing classical statistical baselines against deep learning and zero-shot foundation models. The evaluation explores how these models track heavily augmented 6G network traffic.

## 1. Context: Original vs. Augmented Data
The primary challenge of this project historically stemmed from the coarse, low-resolution nature of the original datasets (e.g., 5-minute averages) which smoothed out underlying transient spikes and burstiness expected in realistic 5G/6G environments. 

By applying an Interrupted Poisson Process (IPP) augmentation strategy (with parameters `tau=0.5` and `zeta=0.5`), the raw 5-minute slots were successfully "unfolded" into rigorous, fine-grained 1-minute (60 seconds) intervals. This synthesis preserves the exact macroscopic cell volumes of the original data whilst simulating massive instantaneous micro-bursts and structural variance.

As a result, the models in this evaluation face a highly difficult, volatile time-series—one much closer to real-world edge traffic.

## 2. Models Evaluated
- **SARIMA**: The classical autoregressive baseline.
- **LSTM**: A deeply-learned sequence model, explicitly trained on scaled previous data traces using PyTorch. 
- **Chronos V1 (amazon/chronos-t5-small)**: Amazon's original zero-shot time series foundation model.
- **Chronos 2 (amazon/chronos-2)**: The current updated zero-shot model architecture.

## 3. Forecast Results & Tracking Performance

The models were benchmarked across two challenging horizons over all 86 unique cell sectors safely using unseen blind tests at the end of the traffic sequence. Performance was measured predominantly using **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)**.

### A. Multi-Step Horizon (12-Step Forecast)
In this scenario, models were given the last 60 minutes of history and tasked to instantly map and predict the entire trajectory of the final 12 minutes without updates.

| Model | Average RMSE | Average Pinball Loss (0.5) |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronos V1** | **10.730** | **4.455** |
| **SARIMA** | 12.026 | 4.946 |
| **Chronos 2** | 12.198 | 5.001 |
| **LSTM** | 15.141 | 6.170 |

* **Analysis**: Chronos V1 exceptionally outperforms everything else here. Generating a 12-step multi-variate trajectory is structurally ideal for Transformer-based topologies. Interestingly, the updated Chronos 2 actually performed marginally worse than its predecessor, coming closely behind the classical SARIMA baseline. The explicitly trained LSTM failed to track the multi-step horizon accurately relative to the foundation models—likely struggling due to the massive local volatility spikes introduced by our IPP algorithm. 

### B. Single-Step Horizon (1-Step Forecast)
In this scenario, the evaluation was strictly measured on the models' ability to track just the absolute final timestamp point.

| Model | Average RMSE | Average Pinball Loss (0.5) |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronos V1** | **9.142** | **4.571** |
| **Chronos 2** | 9.944 | 4.972 |
| **SARIMA** | 11.909 | 5.955 |
| **LSTM** | 24.074 | 12.037 |

* **Analysis**: When confined to a strict next-step horizon, both Large Language Models (Chronos V1 and 2) dominate the classical statistical SARIMA approach. Chronos V1 again maintains the technical lead, scoring a robust ~9.1 RMSE. Conversely, the strict local tracking caused the LSTM's trajectory mechanism to fundamentally break (24.07 RMSE), indicating it simply cannot adjust structurally fast enough to instantaneous sub-minute bursts without over-fitting or blowing up absolute local error.

## 4. Conclusion
1. **Zero-Shot viability is proven**: Even without customized explicit fine-tuning on the augmented dataset, standard Foundation Time Series models inherently contain the macro-economic logic to understand transient telecommunication waves. 
2. **Chronos V1 Outperforms Chronos V2**: In this specific environment—characterized by sudden Poisson-bursts and localized stationary regimes—the newer Chronos 2 variant failed to unseat Chronos V1.  
3. **The Data Augmentation succeeds as a stress-test**: The poor performance of standard LSTMs clearly shows that the new synthetic data is no longer trivially smooth. The generated micro-bursts functionally stress-test architectural reaction-times, forcing modern approaches like Chronos to step in and handle high-variance traffic.
